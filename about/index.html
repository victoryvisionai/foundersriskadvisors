Case Types

Case types we take

We are most useful when the issue is important, narrow, and not easily handled through a routine benefits or wealth planning process. Typical case types include:

Key-person risk

Scoping founder and key-person dependency: what the business loses operationally and financially if a principal is gone, and what coverage is warranted.

Buy-sell funding

Most closely held businesses have a buy-sell agreement. Far fewer have a practical funding mechanism behind it.

Founder dependency

For companies where revenue, relationships, or technical capability concentrate in one or two people.

Executive benefits

Retention risk below the C-suite: whether key leaders, partners and senior producers have adequate protection and retention structures.

Business succession exposure

Continuity planning for founder-led businesses approaching transition, recapitalization, or generational transfer.

Transaction readiness

For companies approaching a raise, recapitalization or sale: where risk, benefits and continuity gaps will surface in diligence.

Process

How the relationship works

01

You flag a case

You identify a situation that may fit Founder Risk Advisors. No client contact is required at this stage. You can describe the situation generally so we can determine whether it is worth discussing further.

02

We scope it together

We have a short call to confirm the issue, the client profile, the likely work required, and whether the case fits. Before any introduction, we agree on the scope, partner roles, expected process, and economics.

03

We agree economics and client contact

The split or referral arrangement is agreed in writing before the first client conversation. We confirm who owns the client communication and how case status is reported back.

04

We support design, placement, or routing

If the case moves forward, we help structure the next step. That may involve key-person planning, buy-sell funding, executive benefits, PEO review, business continuity planning, or routing to the right specialist partner.

Referral Signals

Good referral signals

A founder-led company depends heavily on one person.

A buy-sell agreement exists, but the funding is unclear.

A partner, founder, or key executive is underinsured or uninsured.

A business is preparing for capital, acquisition, succession, or sale.
